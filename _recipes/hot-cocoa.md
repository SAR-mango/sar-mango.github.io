## Ingredients
Makes 2 large mugs of hot cocoa.
- Pistachio liqueur
- Salt
- Heavy whipping cream (optional)
- Chocolate chips (optional)
- Milk
- Cocoa powder (any type)
- Cinnamon powder
- White sugar
- Vanilla extract (optional)

## Recipe
In a saucepan, throw in a good splash (1–2 shots, depending on how nutty you want it to taste) of the pistachio liqueur. Set heat to high; once it starts boiling, reduce to a fast simmer. Then sprinkle in about 2 tbs of the cocoa powder and stir. Wait for the liquid to reduce by around half to ensure the alcohol has boiled off. Then add a pinch of salt.

For a richer hot cocoa, add heavy whipping cream – about the same volume as the reduced liqueur mixture. Set heat to high, stir, then decrease heat to maintain a light simmer. Then mix in 1–2 tbs of chocolate chips and stir thoroughly until melted.

At this point, clumps of chocolate and cocoa powder may remain – this is fine. Now add enough milk to bring the total volume to 2 large mugs. Again, raise the heat to warm up the mixture, but don't let it come to a simmer. It should be just above drinking temperature. Keep stirring and try to break up any clumps.

Once you see small foamy bubbles around the edges of the pot, it's done. Turn off the heat and let the steam dissipate. Then add cinnamon powder (I like a lot) and add a splash of vanilla extract if you wish. Stir, then taste it and add sugar as needed. If you used lots of liqueur and chocolate chips, you shouldn't need to add any sugar at all.

Pour it into mugs through a strainer (to remove remaining clumps) and serve. If you didn't let it get excessively hot, you should be able to add some whipped cream on top if you wish. If it's too hot, hold off on the whipped cream until it cools down a bit; otherwise, the whipped cream will melt immediately.

## Notes
It takes some iteration to get a good feel for the quantities. Don't shy away from adding enough salt; it really brings out the flavors.
